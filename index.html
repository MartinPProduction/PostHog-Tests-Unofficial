    3 · The part that matters

    An incentive lab, not a one-off program

    The goal isn't "launch referrals". It's to discover which incentives work. So the platform ships with a control and four competing predictions, one organisation-level feature flag, and one shared success metric. The arms are all one-sided (only the referrer is rewarded) and priced at a similar value per reward, so a win tells you about the type of incentive, not who got paid.

    One metric, plus the cost

    Activated referred orgs per 100 eligible orgs, per arm. Every assigned org counts, whether or not it created a link (intention-to-treat). Each org gets the same window: 30 days to refer, plus 7 days for the friend to activate. Shares and clicks are read per arm to explain why an arm moved, not to pick the winner.

    Co-decision metric: cost per activated org. Recognition is nearly free and credits are not, so the question is which incentive delivers activated orgs most cheaply.

    Guardrails: incrementality (the control arm shows who would have signed up anyway), referrals that come from the referrer's own organisation, spam and abuse reports, and whether referred orgs retain as well as organic ones.

    Can this experiment detect anything?

    The synthetic funnel above gives about 94 activated referred orgs in 30 days. Split across five arms, that's roughly 20 per arm.

    In a rough simulation (counts treated as Poisson, each incentive compared with control, adjusted for four comparisons), that is enough to reliably spot a lift of about 2.5x. A 2x lift would be caught only about 6 times in 10, and 1.5x only about 1 time in 7.

    So the smallest effect worth detecting has to be big, or the design has to change: fewer arms (control against the two strongest, where 2x is caught about 95% of the time), a longer run, or a pre-agreed leading indicator such as referred sign-ups (about 37 per arm, where 2x is caught about 9 times in 10) to decide faster.

    Decision rule, written before launch
    • A win is an arm that beats control on activated orgs per 100 eligible orgs at the pre-agreed threshold, with cost per activated org inside budget.
    • No arm beats control? That is the finding. Ship the plain link and spend the budget elsewhere.
    • Credits win on activations but lose on cost? Pick the cheapest arm that clears the threshold, and say so in the write-up.
    • No peeking. Arms are not dropped by eye mid-test. If we want to stop arms early, we name a sequential method up front.

    5 · If I got to run it

    Ship small, read results, follow the rule

    Weeks 1–2

    Instrument and check the data

    • Add the four events and group analytics, agree what "activated" means, check the data end to end
    • One placement only (in-app, after the first useful moment)
    Weeks 3–6

    Run five arms at once

    • No incentive, credits, swag, donation, recognition
    • Randomise by organisation, so teammates see the same offer
    • Pre-register the metric, the smallest effect worth detecting and the decision rule, so nobody picks a winner by vibes
    Weeks 7–8

    Decide in public

    • Write up results in the handbook, including the ones that flopped
    • Double down on one arm, add a second surface
    • If no arm beats control, that's the finding: ship the plain link and spend the budget elsewhere
    • Hand the playbook to Sales for partner and customer referrals
